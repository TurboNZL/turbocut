# Layouts, shortcuts and snapping

## Layouts
Settings → Layout. A layout decides where the side panel sits (right, left or bottom sheet), how wide it is, how tall the timeline is, how large the controls are, and which panel tabs show and in what order. Changes apply as you make them.

- Built in: *Automatic* (right panel on a wide screen, sheet on a phone), *Panel on the left*, *Tall timeline*, *Bottom sheet*, *Compact*, *Large controls*.
- **Duplicate to edit** makes your own. Yours can be exported and imported as small files, so a layout can move between devices.
- A narrow window always gets the bottom sheet, because a side panel would crush the preview. It switches back when the window is wide again.

## Shortcuts
Settings → Keys lists every action. Click a shortcut to remove it, **Add** to record another, **Reset** to go back. A conflicting key is reported instead of silently stolen. **?** opens the list.

Useful defaults: **Space** play, **S** split, **[** and **]** trim to the playhead, **K** keyframe, **,** and **.** previous and next keyframe, **N** snapping, **M** mute.

## Cutting and selecting
- Only one thing is selected at a time: a clip **or** a caption. Click empty space on a track, or press **Esc**, to select nothing.
- **Split** (S) cuts at the playhead: the selected clip and its linked picture or sound; the selected caption (between its words, so both halves read right; a one-word caption cannot be split); or, with nothing selected, the clips on the **active track** (the one you last clicked). Click **CC** to make the caption row the active track. Other tracks are left whole.
- **Delete** removes what is selected, never something else.

## Snapping
Clips and edges jump to the playhead and to each other when dragged close. Turn it off with **N**, the magnet button or Settings → General.

## Zoom
Pinch on a touch screen; Ctrl or Alt + mouse wheel on a computer (zooms around the pointer); the zoom buttons in the edit bar. The plain mouse wheel scrolls the timeline sideways; Shift + wheel scrolls the tracks up and down.
